# Fact and source rules

## Evidence levels

- `observed`: directly reproduced or independently documented.
- `vendor-claim`: stated by the maker; attribute it in narration or on screen.
- `inference`: supported capability applied to a workflow; use cautious language.
- `hypothesis`: a proposed test, never a factual headline.

Each claim ledger entry must contain `id`, `text`, `level`, `source_url`, `source_type`, `accessed_at`, `support`, and `boundary`. `source_type` is `official`, `independent`, `community`, or `user-provided`.

Capability claims require an official or independent source. Outcome numbers require comparable observed evidence. Community enthusiasm is not capability evidence. A product being announced does not mean the target audience can access it.

Use readiness consistently:

- `use-now`: generally accessible and core workflow supported by direct evidence.
- `try-now`: accessible but limited by stability, price, region, privacy, or scope.
- `watch`: demo, waitlist, narrow rollout, or insufficient independent evidence.
- `future`: research or prototype with no practical access path.

Claims about the channel itself follow the same rules. Do not say "we test them for you" or "tested for real" unless every case in the video was tested by us. In evergreen copy (a channel intro, a bio, a pinned video) avoid promising a fixed format such as "one-minute videos" or "step by step"; say the value instead, softened where it is approximate ("in about a minute").

Record asset rights separately. Official screenshots may still carry usage conditions. Label AI-generated explanatory images as illustration and never as product proof.
